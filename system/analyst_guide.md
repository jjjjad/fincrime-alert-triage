# Analyst guide

Lessons from cases reviewed by hand, written as general rules. The institution's POLICY.md
always wins where the two differ.

## Ground rules

- A document explains a payment only when payer or payee, amount and date match the transaction.
- A plausible pattern is not evidence. If the document that would prove it is missing, send an RFI
  for that document; do not close.
- Once anything points to crime, escalate. Never send an RFI after the customer has already given
  an explanation the evidence contradicts: asking again risks tipping them off.
- An earlier closed review covers only what it describes. Unrelated contact notes (statement
  requests, charge queries, routine checks) are neither evidence for nor against.

## Signs of crime: escalate

- **Mule activity:** several unrelated people pay in over a few days with mixed references (goods,
  loans, invoices, deposits) that do not fit the customer's job or income, and most leaves within
  48 hours. Fraud reports from sending banks, a customer describing a job forwarding other people's
  money for a cut, crypto outflows, or logins from abroad on a new device each confirm it.
- **Contradictions:** the payment reference, the customer's explanation and where the money went do
  not agree. A payer company that is dissolved is a serious red flag.
- **Structuring:** cash clusters just under a threshold across several branches or shops, beyond what
  the business's accounts support (compare with annual cash sales). A customer asking how to avoid
  reporting or ID checks, or saying they will switch branches, is enough on its own.
- **Higher-risk corridors:** money from several third parties sent straight on, often in identical
  amounts, to several individuals in a higher-risk country is not family support.

## Own accounts

- Own-account money is proven when a statement in the customer's name from the other bank shows the
  same amount and date, and the account number on the payment matches the statement.
- Then apply the 5x rule: below 5 times declared income, close as own account; at 5 times or more,
  send an RFI for an updated profile. No statement, or a different holder name: RFI for source of funds.
- Escalate if the money is quickly sent on to third parties, or follows recent changes to contact
  details or devices.

## Large one-off credits and dormant accounts

- Close when the credit comes directly from a regulated firm (solicitor's client account, insurer,
  employer settlement), its reference and amount match that firm's letter, and it is paid to the
  customer. The payment itself confirms the customer is the beneficiary.
- No letter on file: RFI for source of funds, asking for the letter or agreement.
- On a dormant account, check for takeover signs: changed contact details, new devices or payees,
  quick outflows. A re-verification with a live selfie on the registered device, and money from the
  customer's own account, supports a returning customer.
- A returned tenancy deposit reused for a new holding deposit closes when both letting agents' letters
  match; without them, RFI for the letters.

## Cash deposits and top-ups

- Close when the deposit matches to the pound an earlier cash withdrawal whose purpose was verified.
- Close a one-off private sale when a sale receipt for the same amount is dated just before the
  deposit and an advert or a document shows the customer owned the item.
- Repeated cash just under a threshold closes for a cash business when its records tie each deposit
  to a trading day or site and match to the pound. For a sole trader, the takings book is the business
  record. RFI for business records if the records are missing or do not add up.

## Many payers and money moving on quickly

- Equal payments from several people with one consistent reference are a group collection, not mule
  activity. Close when a booking dated before the money arrived names the payers, the per-person price
  matches, and the total goes to the merchant on the booking. No booking on file: RFI for it.
- A loan spent on its stated purpose is not a pass-through. Close when the loan agreement and the
  merchant invoice both match; without them, RFI for purpose documents.
- Escalate if money goes to individuals or unrelated third parties, references and amounts vary, or
  several loans come in close together.

## Higher-risk countries

- Close a one-off payment straight to an institution (hospital, university, government body) that
  matches its invoice in payee, amount and purpose.
- Close a refund from the same counterparty the customer paid earlier, when that payment was already
  reviewed and the refund matches the confirmation and does not exceed the original.
- RFI for purpose documents if there is no invoice or the payee is not the invoicing body.

## Internal watch list

- A match on one detail alone does not prove it is the same party.
- Close as a different party when the payee's details clearly differ: a different unit in the same
  building, different directors and owners, a different date of birth.
- Name-only match with nothing on file about the payee: RFI for counterparty details (an invoice and
  the payee's business details).
- For a company match, check who controlled it on the payment date, in both the directors' list and
  the ownership register, with appointed, resigned and ceased dates. If the listed person was a
  director or major owner when the payment was made, escalate as a listed party.
